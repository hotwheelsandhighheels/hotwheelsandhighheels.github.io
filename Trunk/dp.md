# The Double Perspective

## Recording a memory 

The first thing we try to do is remember an event exactly as it was -- every detail accounted for, every truth verifiable. We look for evidence: pictures, notes, a ticket stub or receipt. In the digital age, we check screenshots: time stamps, locations (the meta data). 

Sometimes to remember we close our eyes and try to relive the moment through a movie in our brains. Our minds are constantly kneading the memory.

#### Past + Present 

Perhaps our most valuable asset is the passage of time which creates room for <span style="color: var(--concept-retrospection);"> retrospection.</span> If a memory made <span style="color: var(--concept-emotion-authenticity);">an impression</span> on my five year old <span style="color: var(--concept-identity);">self,</span> it would make a different <span style="color: var(--concept-emotion-authenticity);">impression</span> on my 15 year old <span style="color: var(--concept-identity);">self</span> and my 50 year old <span style="color: var(--concept-identity);">self.</span> And each <span style="color: var(--concept-emotion-authenticity);">impression</span> would be a <span style="color: var(--concept-retrospection);">different</span> <span style="color: var(--concept-memory);">copy</span> of <span style="color: var(--concept-situation);">the original event.</span>

#### Reactive + Organized Thought

The <span style="color: var(--concept-character);">character</span> experiencing <span style="color: var(--concept-situation);">the situation</span> is always going to have <span style="color: var(--concept-reactive-thought);">reactive thought</span> because they don’t have the privilege of distance to create <span style="color: var(--concept-retrospection);"> retrospection.</span> With that distance, we are allowed the space to <span style="color: var(--concept-narrator);">organize our thoughts</span> about <span style="color: var(--concept-situation);">the situation</span> and create <span style="color: var(--concept-meaning-story);">meaning.</span> 

The <span style="color: var(--concept-character);">character</span> is allowed to <span style="color: var(--concept-reactive-thought);">be messy</span> and the distance allows us to <span style="color: var(--concept-insight);">be a judge</span> through <span style="color: var(--concept-retrospection);"> retrospection.</span> 

#### Blind spots + Self Awareness

The <span style="color: var(--concept-narrator);">narrator</span> is given the authority to <span style="color: var(--concept-insight);">be a judge</span> because they have developed <span style="color: var(--concept-self-awareness);">self-awareness.</span> The <span style="color: var(--concept-character);">character</span> is not privy to <span style="color: var(--concept-retrospection);">hindsight,</span> they have <span style="color: var(--concept-blind-spot);">blind spots.</span> The <span style="color: var(--concept-narrator);">narrator</span> has the full picture. 

#### Objectivity + Subjectivity

The <span style="color: var(--concept-narrator);">narrator</span> has to allow for <span style="color: var(--concept-body-objectivity);">objectivity</span> in the <span style="color: var(--concept-character);">character</span>. It doesn’t offer any <span style="color: var(--concept-meaning-story);">interpretation</span> of <span style="color: var(--concept-situation);">the situation</span>, it just <span style="color: var(--concept-reactive-thought);">reacts</span> to it.
